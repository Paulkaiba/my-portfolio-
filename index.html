Case study 01

Kelvin Loaf Bakery ordering system

  • Kenya Children’s Homes
  • Team of two
  • In development
  • Client interviews
  • JavaScript, HTML, CSS

Kelvin Loaf Bakery raises income for the children at Kenya Children’s Homes. We are building a place where customers order bread, cakes and pastries online, and where the bakery team runs orders, production and reports.

My role

  • Drew the first wireframes and set the first look of the system.
  • Built the staff accounts, scheduling rules and product catalogue, with role-based access for managers, supervisors and bakers.
  • Work with Nyaga Gacheru, who styled the interface with CSS and builds new features with me.

Working with the bakery’s clients

We stay in touch with the bakery’s clients. We interview them, show them the prototype and check that the ordering flow makes sense to them as we go. What they tell us shapes what we build next.

Case study 02

Pata Kitambulisho (PKMS)

  • Website and Android app
  • Applicants and administrators
  • PHP, MySQL, Kotlin
  • Website complete, app in development

An online platform for applying for national ID services in Kenya. People register, confirm their email with a one-time code, apply for a new, replacement or stolen ID, upload documents, submit payment details and follow their application. Administrators review each application, approve or reject it, post public notices, update homepage content and run reports by date.

My role

  • Built the website and the Android app, working from my own wireframes.
  • Adapted a Bootstrap admin template for ID services.
  • Designed for two kinds of user: applicants, and administrators who can post notices, update the homepage and run reports without touching code.
  • Gladys Njoki wrote the project documentation.

How I work

I design for the people who will use it

I listen first

On the bakery system we interview the bakery’s clients and walk them through the prototype to check the flow works for them.

Clear before clever

Plain language, honest prices like “From Ksh 35”, labelled forms and a clear confirmation at every step.

Safe choices

Ingredients and allergens show before anyone orders, and reset emails say how long a code lasts.

Made for different people

Each role sees only what it needs. Pages reflow on phones, images have alt text and icon buttons have names.

Tested, not assumed

I ran an automated axe-core check. Staff sign-in: 0 issues. Landing page: 2. Ordering app: 2. Automated tools catch only part of the problems, so this is a start.

Honest about gaps

I list what I’m still fixing, so you can see how I think about quality.

Fixing next on the bakery system

  • The item-count badge on the active filter has 3.4:1 contrast. It needs 4.5:1.
  • The ordering app needs a main heading and clearer page landmarks.
  • Buttons need their own visible focus outline for keyboard users.
